# Literature since the 2024 draft — relevance scan (2026-08-11)

## Directly relevant — cite these

- **Lautenbach et al. 2025, *Ecosphere*** — [Landscape features and seasonal
  habitat predict lek site selection and lek size of a Tympanuchus grouse](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecs2.70517).
  Sharp-tailed grouse leks in Saskatchewan sat in **less rugged terrain** with
  **more herbaceous cover**; authors invoke female detection and sound
  propagation. Independent cross-genus corroboration of our negative VRM
  effect and grassland/herbaceous selection. The single most important new
  citation for the Discussion.
- **Milligan et al. 2024, USGS OFR 2024-1018** — [Greater sage-grouse habitat
  of Nevada and northeastern California](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20241018/full):
  broad-scale space use + habitat selection + survival composite for BLM
  planning. Frames our gap statement (their scale: seasonal/landscape; ours:
  lek-site). Companion [raster release v3.0, Sept 2025](https://www.usgs.gov/data/rasters-representing-greater-sage-grouse-space-use-habitat-selection-and-survival-inform)
  is public — candidate **external validation layer**: correlate our
  prediction surface against their selection index as an appendix analysis.
- **USGS OFR 2024-1030** — [Bi-State DPS status evaluation](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20241030/full)
  (trends, habitat selection, conservation efficacy) for the 2024 listing
  review. Status/context citation for the Introduction.
- **Prochazka et al. 2023, *Ecological Indicators*** — targeted annual warning
  system built on lek monitoring. Strengthens the "why lek-scale prediction
  matters to management" argument.

## Currency and framing

- **2025 lek count reports** ([USFWS 2025 results](https://iris.fws.gov/APPS/ServCat/DownloadFile/289678),
  [Utah 2025 lek report](https://wildlife.utah.gov/pdf/sage-grouse/reports/lek-count-report-2025.pdf)) —
  monitoring is ongoing and lek-centric; supports both the relevance claim
  and an explicit statement that our lek-status vintage is 2018 (NDOW),
  refreshable by data request.
- **Conifer-removal response literature, 2023–2025** (e.g., Oregon
  [Conservation Assessment 2025](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/sagegrouse/docs/CAS_Update_2025/Final%20Sage-Grouse%20CAAS%20Sept%202025.pdf);
  [WLFW syntheses](https://www.wlfw.org/how-can-we-balance-sage-grouse-and-pinyon-jay-conservation-in-sagebrush-country/)) —
  females select sites near conifer-removal treatments with improved
  survival; our strong conifer avoidance at leks plugs directly into this
  active management thread (and the pinyon-jay tradeoff debate gives the
  Discussion a live tension to speak to).

## Novelty check

No 2023–2026 publication found on **fine-scale lek site selection for greater
sage-grouse in Nevada**. The nearest work is either broad-scale (Milligan
2024) or different genus/region (Lautenbach 2025). The niche the manuscript
targets remains open, and the newest adjacent result agrees with our
corrected ruggedness finding rather than preempting it.
